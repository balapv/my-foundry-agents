## 1. Purpose

These guidelines describe how Contoso Insurance reviews repair estimates submitted for personal auto physical damage claims. The purpose of estimate review is to confirm that the proposed repairs are necessary to restore the vehicle to its pre-loss condition, that they correspond to damage caused by the reported loss, and that the cost is reasonable.

Estimate review supports the adjuster's handling of the claim. Repair authorization is a separate decision made by authorized claims staff (Section 13).

## 2. Review objectives

For every estimate, the reviewer confirms that:

1. The estimate identifies the correct claim, customer and vehicle.
2. The arithmetic is correct: line amounts, subtotals, materials, taxes and the total.
3. Each repair operation corresponds to damage supported by the claim evidence.
4. Labor times, labor rates, parts and refinish operations are reasonable for the documented damage.
5. The repair approach (repair or replace) is appropriate.
6. Any operation that is not supported, or not clearly supported, is identified and resolved before it is authorized.

## 3. Structure of an estimate

A collision repair estimate normally lists each operation as a separate line, grouped by area of the vehicle. Each line typically shows:

- the operation (for example, replace, repair, refinish, remove and install, blend)
- the part or panel and its side or position (for example, left or right, front or rear)
- the part type (original equipment, aftermarket or recycled) and part price, where applicable
- body, refinish or mechanical labor hours and the labor amount

Estimates also include items that are not tied to a single panel, such as paint and materials, hazardous waste disposal, corrosion protection, calibrations and diagnostic scans. These items are reviewed for reasonableness against the operations they support.

## 4. Labor review

Labor is reviewed for:

- **Labor rate.** The rate should be consistent with the prevailing rate for the repair facility's market.
- **Labor time.** Hours should be consistent with industry estimating guides for the operation. Significant variances should be explained.
- **Overlap.** Labor for adjacent operations should not be counted twice where estimating guides include overlap deductions.
- **Included operations.** Operations already included in another operation's labor time should not be charged separately.

## 5. Parts review

Parts are reviewed for:

- correct identification of the part for the vehicle year, make, model and trim
- correct side and position
- a part type consistent with the policy and state requirements
- a price consistent with current published pricing
- a clear connection between the part and damage supported by the claim evidence

A replacement part is appropriate when the damaged part cannot be safely or economically repaired, or when a repair is not available for that part (for example, many sealed lamp and sensor assemblies are replaced rather than repaired). The fact that a part is expensive, uncommon or unfamiliar is not a reason to question it. The question is whether the evidence supports the damage that the part replaces.

## 6. Refinish review

Refinish operations, blending of adjacent panels and paint and materials are reviewed against the panels being repaired or replaced. Blending adjacent panels to achieve a color match is a normal operation when it is limited to panels next to the repair area. Paint and materials should be proportionate to the refinish labor on the estimate.

## 7. Repair versus replace

The reviewer considers whether a damaged panel or component should be repaired or replaced, based on:

- the type, location and severity of the damage
- manufacturer repair guidance, including any restrictions on repairing structural or safety components
- the relative cost of repair and replacement, including refinish

When an estimate replaces a component that could reasonably be repaired, or repairs a component that should be replaced for safety reasons, the reviewer discusses the approach with the repair facility.

## 8. Estimate and evidence consistency

Each repair operation should correspond to damage that the claim evidence connects to the reported loss. The reviewer compares the estimate with:

- the damaged areas reported in the claim form
- the areas of impact and damage recorded in the police report, when there is one
- the damage visible in the submitted photographs
- the mechanism of the loss (for example, the direction and point of impact)

The comparison is performed line by line, or by group of related lines for the same area. For each line or group, the reviewer records whether it is:

| Category | Meaning | Treatment |
|---|---|---|
| Supported | The damage the line repairs is reported, documented or visible, and is consistent with how the loss occurred | Continue normal review |
| Supported as a related operation | The line is a necessary consequence of a supported repair (for example, removing an adjacent part for access, blending an adjacent panel, a calibration required after replacing a sensor-mounted part) | Continue normal review |
| Not established by current evidence | No source in the claim file connects the damage the line repairs to the reported loss, or the sources conflict | Request supporting information before authorization (Section 10) |

Damage that is consistent with the mechanism of the loss can be supported even if one source does not mention it. Components behind or beneath a documented damaged area — for example a bumper reinforcement or energy absorber behind a damaged bumper cover, or measuring and straightening for a deformed panel — are commonly written on an initial estimate from the visible damage and the point of impact. They are treated as supported when they are in the documented impact area and consistent with how the loss occurred; the repair facility confirms them during disassembly, and any change is handled as a supplement (Section 9). Damage that is first found during disassembly, and was not written on the original estimate, is documented through a supplement with teardown photographs.

## 9. Supplements

A supplement is an additional estimate submitted after repairs begin, usually because hidden damage is found during disassembly. Supplements are reviewed in the same way as the original estimate. Supplement lines should be supported by teardown photographs or a description of the hidden damage and its relationship to the original impact.

## 10. Line items not established by the current evidence

### 10.1 Identifying the item

A line item is not established by the current evidence when the claim file does not connect the damage it repairs to the reported loss. Common situations include:

- a repair to an area of the vehicle that is not mentioned in the claim form, is not recorded in the police report and is not shown in the photographs
- a repair to an area away from the reported point of impact, with no description of how the loss caused that damage
- photographs that show the area only from a distance or at an angle that does not allow the reviewer to see the condition of the component
- a repair that appears to duplicate an operation from a prior claim for the same area

### 10.2 How to treat the item

When a line item is not established by the current evidence, the reviewer:

1. Identifies the specific line, its amount and the area of the vehicle it relates to.
2. States which sources were compared and what each one shows about that area.
3. Considers ordinary explanations, such as a reporting omission, damage found after disassembly, secondary damage from the impact, a data-entry error on the estimate or a photograph that does not cover the area.
4. Requests the information needed to resolve the question (Section 12).
5. Places that line item in manual review and does not authorize it until it is resolved.
6. Continues the review of the supported lines on the estimate.

An unsupported line item is a question to be answered. It is not, by itself, evidence that the customer or the repair facility has acted improperly. Reviewers do not describe a line item, estimate, customer or repair facility as fraudulent. Escalation beyond ordinary review is governed by the Claims Escalation and Special Investigation Guidance.

### 10.3 What not to conclude

The reviewer should not conclude that damage does not exist merely because the available evidence does not show it. The correct finding is that the current evidence does not establish the damage or its connection to the loss. The finding changes if supporting evidence is received.

## 11. Limits of photographic evidence

Photographs are often the best available evidence of damage, but they have limits:

- They show only what is in frame. An area that is not photographed, or is photographed from a distance, has not been documented.
- Lighting, reflections, angle and resolution can hide or exaggerate damage.
- Some damage, such as cracked internal mounts, broken brackets, sensor faults or internal lamp failures, is not visible from the outside.
- A general view in which no damage is apparent does not prove that an area is undamaged.

When the reviewer relies on photographs, the finding should describe what the photographs show and do not show, using qualified language such as "the submitted photographs show..." or "the submitted photographs do not show...". When a determination depends on detail that the photographs cannot provide, request additional close-up photographs, teardown photographs or an inspection.

## 12. Requesting support and revised estimates

When additional information is needed, the request should identify the specific line or lines, explain what is needed and ask for one or more of the following:

- close-up photographs of the component or area
- teardown or diagnostic findings that show the damage and how the loss caused it
- an explanation from the repair facility of the relationship between the damage and the reported loss
- a corrected or revised estimate, if the line was included in error
- a field or virtual inspection

When a revised estimate is received, the reviewer compares it with the earlier estimate and confirms that every change is explained.

## 13. Authorization boundaries

Estimate review produces findings and recommendations. It does not, by itself, authorize repairs or payment.

- Repairs are authorized by claims staff within their authority level after estimate review is complete for the lines being authorized.
- Supported lines may be authorized while a line item that is not established remains in review, at the adjuster's discretion.
- A line item that is not established by the evidence is not authorized until it is resolved or removed.
- Decision-support tools may summarize the estimate, compare it with the evidence and recommend next steps. They do not authorize repairs or approve estimates.

## 14. Continuing supported processing

A question about one line item should not delay the parts of the claim that are supported. The adjuster may continue processing supported repairs according to the policy and authority levels while the open item is resolved, and tells the customer and the repair facility which item is in review and what is needed.

## 15. Documentation

The estimate review is documented in the claim file, including:

- the estimate reviewed, its date and total
- confirmation of the arithmetic
- the sources compared
- each line item placed in review, the reason and the information requested
- the outcome of each request and any revised estimate
