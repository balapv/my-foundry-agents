## 1. Purpose

This guidance explains when a claim question should be escalated beyond ordinary claims handling, the difference between ordinary manual review and a Special Investigations Unit (SIU) referral, and the standards of conduct that apply when a claim raises questions.

Most questions that arise during claim handling are resolved through ordinary review: the adjuster identifies the question, requests information and makes a decision when the information is received. Escalation is used when ordinary review is not sufficient. A referral to Special Investigations is used only when the criteria in Section 6 are met.

## 2. Principles

- **Questions are normal.** Missing documents, unclear details and estimate questions occur in many legitimate claims. They are resolved through ordinary review.
- **Indicators are not conclusions.** An indicator listed in this guidance is a reason to look more closely. It is not a finding that anyone has acted improperly.
- **No accusations.** Claims staff, and the tools they use, do not accuse customers, claimants, repair facilities or other parties of fraud, dishonesty or misrepresentation.
- **Proportionate response.** The response should match the question. A single unexplained repair line is normally handled by a request for supporting information, not by an investigation.
- **People decide.** Escalation and referral decisions are made by authorized staff.

## 3. Ordinary manual review

Ordinary manual review is part of normal claims handling. It is appropriate when:

- required evidence is missing or incomplete
- an estimate line item is not established by the current evidence
- documents contain an inconsistency that has an ordinary possible explanation
- the date of loss or coverage needs to be confirmed
- photographs do not adequately show the damaged area

In ordinary manual review, the adjuster requests the specific information needed, documents the request and continues handling the supported parts of the claim. Ordinary manual review does not require supervisor approval and is not reported to Special Investigations.

## 4. Supervisor escalation

A claim is escalated to a claims supervisor when:

- the decision required is above the adjuster's authority level
- a coverage question cannot be resolved from the policy record
- a material inconsistency remains after the adjuster has requested and reviewed clarification
- the customer disputes the handling of the claim
- the adjuster believes the claim may meet the criteria for an SIU referral

The supervisor decides whether the claim continues in ordinary review, requires coverage review or should be referred to Special Investigations.

## 5. Indicators that may warrant closer review

The following situations may warrant closer review. Each has many possible innocent explanations, and none is, by itself, a basis for an SIU referral.

### 5.1 Material inconsistencies

A material inconsistency is a conflict between sources about a fact that affects coverage or the amount of the loss, such as the date of loss, the vehicle involved, the parties involved or the cause of the damage. A material inconsistency is first handled through a request for clarification. It becomes an escalation question when it cannot be explained after clarification is requested.

### 5.2 Possible duplicate claims

A possible duplicate exists when two claims appear to describe the same loss, for example the same vehicle, a similar date and the same damaged area. Possible duplicates are confirmed by comparing the claim records, documents and photographs. Many apparent duplicates are administrative, such as a claim opened twice or a claim reported by both parties. When a duplicate is confirmed, the claims are consolidated; when it is not, both proceed separately.

### 5.3 Conflicting dates

Dates that differ between the claim record, the customer's account, the police report and the repair records are clarified with the customer and, where relevant, the reporting agency. Coverage is not confirmed while the date of loss is in question. A date conflict that changes whether the policy was in force on the date of loss is escalated for coverage review.

### 5.4 Repeated losses

A customer's prior claim history is relevant when it relates to the current loss, for example a prior claim for damage to the same area that may not have been repaired. Prior claims are a normal part of insurance. A history of several claims is not, by itself, an indicator of misrepresentation and is not used as a reason to question an otherwise supported claim.

### 5.5 Questions about the authenticity of documents

When a document or photograph appears to have been altered, appears to relate to a different vehicle, or contains details that cannot be reconciled with other records, the adjuster records the specific observation and escalates to a supervisor. The adjuster does not confront the customer or repair facility about the authenticity of a document.

### 5.6 Policy issues

Losses shortly after a policy is issued or reinstated, or shortly before or after a cancellation, are reviewed for coverage. Timing alone is not an indicator of misrepresentation.

### 5.7 Conflicts between systems

When the claims system, policy system or a third-party record disagree, the adjuster records the conflict and asks the system owner to confirm the correct record. A system conflict is treated as a data question unless there is evidence otherwise.

## 6. Criteria for a Special Investigations referral

A claim is referred to Special Investigations when, after ordinary review and supervisor escalation, there is a specific and articulable basis to believe that a material fact may have been misrepresented. Examples include:

- evidence that a document or photograph has been altered
- a confirmed duplicate submission for the same loss after clarification
- a material inconsistency that remains unexplained after clarification has been requested and reviewed
- information from a law enforcement agency, another insurer or an industry database indicating a possible organized scheme

A referral requires supervisor approval. The referral documents the specific facts, the sources and the clarification already requested. A referral is not a finding of fraud; it is a request for specialized review.

## 7. When not to escalate

Escalation or referral is not appropriate when:

- the only question is a missing document or photograph that has not yet been requested
- a single estimate line item is not yet supported and supporting information has not been requested
- the customer has prior claims that are unrelated to the current loss
- the loss amount is high but supported by the evidence
- a repair item is unusual or expensive but supported by the evidence
- an inconsistency has an ordinary explanation that has not yet been explored

In these situations the adjuster continues ordinary review.

## 8. Ordinary review compared with Special Investigations

| | Ordinary manual review | Special Investigations referral |
|---|---|---|
| Purpose | Resolve a specific question so the claim can proceed | Specialized review of a possible material misrepresentation |
| Trigger | Missing, incomplete or inconsistent evidence | Specific, articulable basis after ordinary review and escalation |
| Approval | Adjuster | Supervisor approval required |
| Typical action | Request photographs, documents, clarification or a revised estimate | SIU case opened by SIU staff |
| Effect on the claim | Affected item is held; supported parts continue | Handling coordinated with SIU |
| Communication | Customer told what is needed and why | Communications coordinated with SIU and compliance |

## 9. Special Investigations authority

Only the Special Investigations Unit opens investigation cases. Only authorized SIU and claims leadership may make findings about misrepresentation. Adjusters and decision-support tools do not open investigations, make findings about fraud or tell any party that a claim is under investigation.

## 10. Decision-support tools

Decision-support tools, including AI assistants used by claims staff, may:

- identify inconsistencies and evidence gaps
- explain which sources were compared
- recommend ordinary manual review and specific information requests
- note that a situation meets one of the indicators in Section 5, for the adjuster's consideration

Decision-support tools must not:

- describe a customer, claimant or repair facility as fraudulent or dishonest
- recommend an SIU referral on the basis of an unresolved question that has not been through ordinary review
- open, or state that they have opened, an investigation
- approve, deny or authorize any part of a claim

## 11. Customer and repair facility communication

Communications about open questions are factual and courteous. They state what information is needed and why, without implying wrongdoing. When a claim is referred to Special Investigations, communications are coordinated with SIU and compliance. Staff do not tell any party that they are suspected of fraud.

## 12. Documentation

Every escalation and referral is documented with:

- the specific question or indicator
- the sources reviewed
- the clarification requested and the response
- the decision made, by whom and why

Documentation uses neutral, factual language.
