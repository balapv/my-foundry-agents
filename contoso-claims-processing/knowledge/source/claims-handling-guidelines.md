## 1. Purpose and scope

These guidelines describe how Contoso Insurance claims staff receive, review, document and progress personal auto physical damage claims. They apply to collision, comprehensive and property damage liability claims for private passenger vehicles insured under a Contoso Insurance personal auto policy.

The guidelines set the minimum standard of review. They do not replace the policy contract, state regulations or the more detailed guidance in the companion documents:

- **Personal Auto Coverage Guidance** (CI-CLM-CV-204) explains how coverages, deductibles and exclusions are applied.
- **Repair Estimate Review Guidelines** (CI-CLM-ER-310) explains how repair estimates are reviewed against claim evidence.
- **Claims Escalation and Special Investigation Guidance** (CI-CLM-SI-420) explains when and how to escalate a claim beyond ordinary review.

Where these guidelines and the policy contract differ, the policy contract and applicable law control. Where the correct treatment is unclear, the adjuster seeks guidance from a claims supervisor rather than guessing.

## 2. Guiding principles

Every claim is handled according to the same principles, regardless of the amount, the customer or the repair facility involved.

- **Evidence before conclusions.** Statements about what happened, what was damaged and what is owed are based on the claim file, not on assumptions.
- **Policy before coverage statements.** Coverage, deductibles and conditions are confirmed against the policy record in force on the date of loss before they are communicated.
- **Consistency is checked, not assumed.** The claim form, police report, photographs, repair estimate and structured records are compared with each other.
- **Discrepancies trigger review, not conclusions.** A discrepancy is a reason to obtain clarification. It is not, by itself, evidence of misrepresentation or fraud.
- **People make consequential decisions.** Payment, denial, repair authorization and referral decisions are made by authorized claims staff, as described in Section 13.
- **Fair, timely and respectful handling.** Customers and repair facilities are treated courteously and are told clearly what is needed and why.

## 3. Claim intake (first notice of loss)

A claim is opened when a policyholder, an authorized representative or a third party reports a loss through any supported channel (online, mobile, phone or agent). At intake, the following information is captured:

- policy number and named insured
- the insured vehicle involved, identified by the vehicle record on the policy
- date, time and location of the loss
- type of loss (for example, collision with another vehicle, single-vehicle collision, theft, weather, glass)
- the policyholder's description of how the loss occurred
- the areas of the vehicle the policyholder reports as damaged
- whether the vehicle is drivable
- whether anyone was injured
- other parties involved, their vehicles and their insurers, if known
- whether the loss was reported to police and, if so, the agency and report number
- the repair facility selected by the customer, if any

The intake description is the customer's account. It is recorded as provided. Claims staff do not edit the description to fit later evidence; any later clarification is added as a separate, dated note.

## 4. Evidence requirements

### 4.1 Standard evidence

Most physical damage claims are supported by the following evidence:

| Evidence | Normally expected | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Claim form or first notice of loss | Always | Customer account of the loss, reported damage and parties |
| Photographs of the damage | Always for physical damage | Visual record of the damaged areas and the vehicle's condition |
| Repair estimate | Before repairs are authorized | Proposed repair operations, parts, labor and cost |
| Police report | See Section 4.2 | Independent account of the incident from the responding agency |
| Other party information | When another party is involved | Liability evaluation and subrogation |
| Proof of ownership or loan information | When a total loss or lienholder payment is possible | Correct payee identification |

### 4.2 When a police report is expected

A police report is expected, and is requested when not provided, when any of the following apply:

- another vehicle or another party's property was involved and the parties disagree about what happened
- the customer reports that police responded to the scene
- anyone was injured
- the loss involves theft, vandalism or a hit-and-run
- the reported damage exceeds the state reporting threshold

When the customer reports that a police report exists, the claim file should contain the report, or a documented request for it, before the claim is finalized. A missing police report is recorded as an evidence gap. Claims staff do not describe the contents of a police report that has not been received.

### 4.3 Photograph standards

Photographs should allow a reviewer to identify the vehicle and see the damaged areas. A useful photo set normally includes:

- at least one overall view of each side of the vehicle that is reported as damaged
- close views of each damaged area
- a view that shows the vehicle's identity (for example, the license plate or a distinctive feature) where practical
- views of other areas of the vehicle when repairs to those areas are requested

See Section 8 for the limits of what photographs can establish.

## 5. Initial review

Initial review is completed within two business days of the first notice of loss, or as soon as the necessary records are available. During initial review the adjuster:

1. Confirms the claim record identifies the correct customer, policy and vehicle.
2. Confirms the policy status and coverage in force on the date of loss (Section 6 and Section 7).
3. Reviews the customer's account and the reported damage.
4. Identifies the evidence already received and the evidence still expected.
5. Reviews the customer's prior claim history only when it is relevant to the current loss, for example to identify a possible duplicate submission or unrepaired prior damage to the same area.
6. Records the review in the claim file and requests any missing information.

## 6. Date-of-loss validation

The date of loss determines which policy term, coverages and deductibles apply. The adjuster confirms that:

- the date of loss on the claim record matches the date in the customer's account
- the date of loss is consistent with the police report and other dated evidence
- the date of loss falls within a policy term in which the policy was in force

When dated sources disagree (for example, the claim record and the police report give different dates), the adjuster does not choose one date without support. The discrepancy is recorded and the customer, the reporting agency or both are asked to clarify. Coverage is not confirmed until the date of loss is established. Material, unexplained date conflicts are handled under the Claims Escalation and Special Investigation Guidance.

## 7. Coverage verification

Coverage is verified against the policy record, not against the customer's description of their policy. The adjuster confirms:

- the policy status on the date of loss (active, expired, cancelled or otherwise not in force)
- that the vehicle involved is listed on the policy for the relevant term
- that the coverage applicable to the type of loss (for example, collision or comprehensive) is listed for that vehicle
- the deductible that applies to that coverage
- any lienholder or loss payee who must be included on payments

How coverages, deductibles and exclusions are applied is described in the Personal Auto Coverage Guidance. When coverage cannot be confirmed from the available records, the adjuster records the reason and refers the question for coverage review. The adjuster does not tell a customer that a loss is covered, or not covered, until coverage has been verified.

## 8. Reviewing photographs

Photographs are a primary source of evidence about the location, type and severity of physical damage. Adjusters review each photograph and record what it shows.

When reviewing photographs:

- Describe what is visible: the area of the vehicle, the type of damage (for example, dent, scrape, crack, broken component) and its approximate extent.
- Compare the visible damage with the customer's account, the police report and the repair estimate.
- Note the limits of each image, including distance, angle, lighting, resolution, obstruction and whether the area of interest is in frame.

Photographs establish what they show. They do not establish what they do not show. An area that is not visible, is out of focus or is photographed only from a distance has not been shown to be undamaged. In the same way, an overall view in which no damage is apparent is not proof that minor or concealed damage does not exist. When a repair is requested for an area that the available photographs do not adequately show, the appropriate response is to request additional photographs or an inspection, not to conclude that the damage does not exist.

## 9. Reviewing police reports

A police report is an independent account prepared by the responding agency. The adjuster reviews the report for:

- the date, time and location of the incident
- the vehicles and parties involved
- the narrative and any diagram of the collision
- the areas of impact and damage recorded for each vehicle
- any citations, contributing factors or witness statements

The police report is compared with the customer's account and the other evidence. A police report that records damage in one area does not, by itself, exclude damage elsewhere; responding officers record what they observe at the scene. However, when a repair is requested for an area that no part of the claim evidence associates with the loss, the request needs supporting evidence (Section 12).

## 10. Reviewing repair estimates

Repair estimates are reviewed under the Repair Estimate Review Guidelines before repairs are authorized. At a minimum, the adjuster confirms that:

- the estimate identifies the correct vehicle and claim
- the estimate arithmetic is correct
- each repair operation corresponds to damage that is supported by the claim evidence
- the parts, labor and refinish operations are reasonable for the documented damage

Estimates are compared line by line with the reported damage, the police report and the photographs. Most estimates contain operations that are clearly supported and, occasionally, operations that need clarification. Each line item is considered on its own evidence.

## 11. Inconsistent or incomplete evidence

Evidence is inconsistent when two sources describe the loss differently, or when a requested repair is not supported by the evidence received. Examples include different dates of loss on two documents, repair operations for an area that no claim document associates with the reported incident, or photographs that do not show the area being repaired.

When evidence is inconsistent or incomplete, the adjuster:

1. Identifies the specific inconsistency or gap and the sources involved.
2. Considers ordinary explanations, such as an omission at intake, a typing error, damage that is only visible after disassembly, or a photograph that does not cover the relevant area.
3. Requests the information needed to resolve it (Section 12).
4. Places the affected portion of the claim in manual review until it is resolved.
5. Continues handling the parts of the claim that are supported and not affected by the inconsistency.

An inconsistency is not a finding of misrepresentation. Claims staff must not describe a customer or repair facility as dishonest, or a claim as fraudulent, because of an unresolved inconsistency.

## 12. Requests for supplemental evidence

Supplemental evidence is requested when it is needed to resolve a specific question. Requests should be specific, explain what is needed and why, and be addressed to the party best able to provide it.

| Question to resolve | Typical request | Usually directed to |
|---|---|---|
| Damage to an area is not shown in submitted photographs | Additional photographs of the area, or an inspection | Customer or repair facility |
| A repair line item is not supported by the claim evidence | Supporting photographs, teardown findings or a corrected estimate | Repair facility |
| A police report is referenced but not received | Copy of the report | Customer or reporting agency |
| Dates or details conflict between documents | Clarification and any supporting documentation | Customer, reporting agency or both |
| Ownership or lienholder information is unclear | Title or lender information | Customer |

Requests and responses are documented in the claim file with the date sent and the date received.

## 13. Manual review and decision authority

### 13.1 Manual review

Manual review means that a qualified adjuster examines a specific question before the claim proceeds on that point. Manual review is ordinary claims handling. It is used, for example, when:

- coverage or the date of loss cannot be confirmed from the records
- a repair line item is not supported by the current evidence
- documents conflict on a material fact
- required evidence is missing
- an automated or assisted review identifies a question it cannot resolve

Manual review of one issue does not stop the review of unaffected parts of the claim. For example, supported repair operations can continue through normal review while an unsupported line item is clarified.

### 13.2 Decision authority

The following actions require a decision by authorized claims staff acting within their authority level:

- confirming coverage to a customer
- authorizing repairs or approving a repair estimate
- issuing any payment
- denying a claim or any part of a claim
- referring a claim to Special Investigations
- closing a claim

Decision-support tools, including AI assistants, may summarize evidence, identify questions and recommend next steps. They do not approve, deny, authorize or pay claims, and they do not refer claims to Special Investigations. A recommendation from a decision-support tool is reviewed by the responsible adjuster before any action is taken.

## 14. Escalation

Most questions are resolved within ordinary manual review. A claim is escalated to a claims supervisor when:

- the adjuster's authority level is exceeded
- coverage is in question and cannot be resolved from the policy record
- the customer disputes a coverage position
- documents conflict on a material fact and clarification has not resolved the conflict

Criteria for referral to Special Investigations, and the difference between ordinary review and formal investigation, are defined in the Claims Escalation and Special Investigation Guidance.

## 15. Documentation requirements

The claim file must allow another adjuster to understand what was reviewed, what was concluded and why. For each claim, document:

- the evidence received and the date it was received
- the coverage verification and its source
- the comparison of the estimate with the claim evidence, including any line items placed in review
- each request for supplemental evidence and its outcome
- each decision, who made it and the evidence it relied on

File notes describe facts and open questions in neutral language. They do not speculate about motive or character.

## 16. Customer communication

Customers are told what has been confirmed, what is still needed and what happens next. When additional evidence is requested, the request explains what is needed and why in plain language. When part of a claim is in review, the customer is told which part and what will resolve it. Communications do not imply that the customer or the repair facility has acted improperly.
