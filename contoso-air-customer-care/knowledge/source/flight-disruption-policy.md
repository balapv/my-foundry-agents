## 1. Purpose

1.1 This policy sets out the standards Contoso Air Customer Care applies when a Contoso Air flight is cancelled, significantly delayed or subject to a schedule change, and when a customer asks to change a booking. It defines when passengers may be reaccommodated without a change fee, how alternatives are selected, how traveling parties and cabins are handled, when refunds are available and when a case must be escalated, so that every customer receives the same answer in every channel.

## 2. Scope

2.1 This policy applies to all Customer Care channels and teams, including Tier 1 Customer Care, Tier 2 Customer Care Specialists and Supervisors, the Irregular Operations (IROPS) Recovery Desk, and airport customer service staff when they perform reaccommodation. It applies to tickets issued by Contoso Air for travel on Contoso Air-operated flights.

2.2 Meal and hotel assistance is governed by the Meal and Hotel Assistance Policy (CA-CC-POL-021). Loyalty benefits are governed by Contoso Air Altitude Loyalty Benefits (CA-LOY-POL-003). Escalation procedures and authority limits are governed by the Customer Service Escalation Guide (CA-CC-GDE-007).

2.3 Where regulations at an international station provide passengers with greater entitlements than this policy, those regulations prevail for the flights to which they apply.

## 3. Definitions

3.1 Carrier-initiated cancellation: a Contoso Air flight cancelled by Contoso Air for any reason. A carrier-initiated cancellation is either a controllable disruption or an uncontrollable disruption.

3.2 Controllable disruption: a cancellation or delay caused by circumstances within Contoso Air's control, including crew availability, aircraft maintenance, operational disruption, IT systems and aircraft swaps.

3.3 Uncontrollable disruption: a cancellation or delay caused by circumstances outside Contoso Air's control, including weather, air traffic control, airport closures, security events and government orders.

3.4 Significant delay: a departure delay of 3 hours or more compared with the scheduled departure time.

3.5 Schedule change: a change to the time or routing of a Contoso Air flight that Contoso Air notifies to the customer before the day of departure.

3.6 Reservation or traveling party: all passengers booked on the same reservation (confirmation code).

3.7 Primary traveler: the passenger who holds or made the reservation.

3.8 Eligible alternative: a Contoso Air-operated flight between the same origin and destination, departing on the original travel date or the following calendar day, nonstop or with at most one connection, with confirmed seat availability.

3.9 Cabin: the class of service on a Contoso Air flight. From lowest to highest, the cabins are Main Cabin, Economy Plus and First.

3.10 Customer-requested (voluntary) change: a change the customer chooses to make while the booked flight is operating as scheduled.

3.11 Comparable cabin: the cabin booked on the original itinerary. A higher cabin provided under Section 10.3 is treated as comparable. A lower cabin is a downgrade and is handled under Section 10.4.

3.12 Customer Care tiers: Tier 1 Customer Care (frontline agents and the Contoso Air digital assistant); Tier 2 Customer Care Specialists and Supervisors; the IROPS Recovery Desk; Accessibility Services; the Unaccompanied Minor Desk; and the International Travel Documentation Desk.

## 4. Airline-Initiated Cancellations

4.1 Every Contoso Air flight cancelled by Contoso Air is a carrier-initiated cancellation, whether the cause is controllable or uncontrollable. The classification affects only eligibility for meal and hotel assistance under CA-CC-POL-021. It does not affect a passenger's entitlement to reaccommodation, the change-fee waiver or a refund under this policy.

4.2 Customer Care must rely on the disruption reason code recorded in the operational system and must not speculate about the cause. If no reason code is recorded, Customer Care must tell the customer that the cause is still being confirmed.

4.3 The reservation system may automatically rebook affected passengers. Customers may accept the automatic rebooking or ask for a different eligible alternative.

### 4.4 Core Reaccommodation Rule

When a flight is cancelled by Contoso Air, affected passengers may be reaccommodated on an eligible Contoso Air alternative without a change fee. When multiple passengers are traveling on the same reservation, Customer Care should attempt to keep the traveling party together.

4.4.1 This rule is applied in accordance with Sections 5 to 10. The obligations for keeping a traveling party together are set out in Section 9.

## 5. Rebooking Eligibility

5.1 A passenger is eligible for reaccommodation under this policy when the passenger holds a confirmed ticket on a Contoso Air-operated flight that is affected by any of the following:

- a carrier-initiated cancellation, whether controllable or uncontrollable;
- a significant delay;
- a qualifying schedule change as defined in Section 13.4; or
- a missed connection on the same reservation caused by the late arrival of a Contoso Air-operated flight.

5.2 Reaccommodation under this policy is limited to eligible alternatives as defined in Section 3.8. Every condition in that definition must be met, including confirmed seat availability for every passenger being rebooked. Options that do not meet the definition, such as a different airport, two or more connections, or a departure more than one calendar day after the original travel date, may be considered only by the IROPS Recovery Desk following escalation.

5.3 Reaccommodation on other airlines is available only through the IROPS Recovery Desk. Tier 1 Customer Care must not offer, promise or imply rebooking on another airline.

5.4 Contoso Air Altitude members may receive priority in the reaccommodation queue as set out in CA-LOY-POL-003. Priority does not guarantee a seat.

5.5 Customer Care must not confirm a rebooking until the change has been completed in the reservation system and the customer has explicitly agreed to the proposed itinerary. Before making the change, Customer Care must describe the proposed flights, times, connection, cabin and passengers included.

## 6. Change-Fee Waiver

6.1 For carrier-initiated cancellations, whether controllable or uncontrollable, and for significant delays, the change fee is waived and no fare difference is charged when a passenger is reaccommodated on an eligible alternative.

6.2 The waiver applies to every passenger on the reservation who is affected by the disruption, regardless of fare type, including Basic fares.

6.3 The waiver applies only to reaccommodation on an eligible alternative. If a customer chooses a flight that is not an eligible alternative, for example travel on a later date, the change is a customer-requested change under Section 12 and the fare rules apply, unless the IROPS Recovery Desk authorizes otherwise.

6.4 Delays of less than 3 hours do not qualify for the waiver, except where the delay causes a missed connection on the same reservation under Section 5.1.

## 7. Same-Day Alternatives

7.1 Customer Care must offer same-day eligible alternatives first.

7.2 Next-day eligible alternatives may be offered only when no same-day eligible alternative accommodates the whole traveling party, or when the customer requests travel on the following day.

7.3 Customer Care should first present the eligible alternative with the earliest arrival that keeps the traveling party together. For each option, Customer Care must state the local departure and arrival times, any connection point and the cabin.

7.4 Customer Care must not place a passenger on standby as a substitute for a confirmed eligible alternative.

## 8. Connecting Versus Nonstop Itineraries

8.1 When the original itinerary was nonstop, Customer Care should prefer a nonstop eligible alternative when it accommodates the whole traveling party and departs and arrives at a comparable time.

8.2 A connecting eligible alternative is appropriate when it is the only same-day option that keeps the traveling party together, or when it arrives materially earlier than the available nonstop alternative.

8.3 When offering a connecting alternative in place of a nonstop itinerary, Customer Care must explain the tradeoff, including the connection, the longer travel time and the arrival time. The customer decides which option to accept.

8.4 The minimum connection time at Contoso Air domestic hubs is 45 minutes. Customer Care must not offer a connection shorter than the minimum connection time.

8.5 An unaccompanied minor must never be rebooked onto a connecting itinerary or onto the last flight of the day without approval from the Unaccompanied Minor Desk, as described in CA-CC-GDE-007.

## 9. Keeping Passengers on the Same Reservation Together

9.1 Customer Care must rebook all passengers on the same reservation onto the same itinerary whenever seats are available for the whole traveling party.

9.2 Customer Care must not split a traveling party without the explicit consent of the primary traveler.

9.3 A child under 15 must never be separated from at least one accompanying adult on the same reservation. This rule applies even when the primary traveler consents to splitting the party.

9.4 If no eligible alternative accommodates the whole traveling party, Customer Care must:

- explain to the customer that no eligible alternative currently accommodates the whole party;
- offer the earliest eligible alternative that keeps the party together, including an alternative on the following calendar day; and
- offer escalation to the IROPS Recovery Desk.

9.5 Splitting the traveling party may be discussed only as an option chosen by the customer, and always subject to Section 9.3. A split may be completed only by a Tier 2 Customer Care Specialist or Supervisor or by the IROPS Recovery Desk, after the primary traveler has given explicit consent. The consent and the resulting itineraries must be recorded in the reservation notes.

9.6 Passengers traveling together on different reservations are separate traveling parties. The protections in Sections 9.1 to 9.5 apply to each reservation separately.

## 10. Cabin Handling

10.1 Passengers must be reaccommodated in the same cabin as the original booking when that cabin is available.

10.2 If the same cabin is not available on an eligible alternative for the whole traveling party, Customer Care may seat passengers in the next available cabin.

10.3 When the next available cabin is higher than the original cabin, it is provided at no additional charge. This is not an upgrade benefit, is not recorded as a complimentary upgrade and creates no entitlement on any other flight.

10.4 When the next available cabin is lower than the original cabin, Customer Care must obtain the customer's consent before making the change. The fare difference for the affected segment is refunded to the original form of payment.

10.5 Upgrades are never offered as compensation for a disruption.

10.6 Seat availability is assessed by cabin. Seats available in different cabins cannot be combined to treat a flight as offering comparable seating for the whole traveling party. On a connecting itinerary, the seats available in a cabin are the lowest number available in that cabin on any flight in the itinerary.

10.7 Passengers on the same reservation may be seated in different cabins on the same itinerary only with the consent of the primary traveler. Each passenger seated in a lower cabin is handled under Section 10.4. Seating passengers in different cabins on the same flights is not a split of the traveling party under Section 9, but Section 9.3 continues to apply to seating arrangements for children under 15.

## 11. Refund Eligibility

11.1 For carrier-initiated cancellations and significant delays, a customer who declines all eligible alternatives is entitled to a full refund of the unused portion of the ticket to the original form of payment, regardless of fare type.

11.2 As an alternative to a refund, the customer may choose a Contoso Air travel credit for the value of the unused portion of the ticket.

11.3 Refunds are processed by the Refunds team after the customer requests a refund. Customer Care may explain refund eligibility and help the customer submit a request, but must not state that a refund has been approved or issued until the Refunds team has processed it.

11.4 Refunds to a credit or debit card are processed within 7 business days in accordance with payment rules. Refunds to other forms of payment follow the processing times of the applicable payment provider.

11.5 Customer-requested cancellations are governed by the fare rules and do not qualify for refunds under Section 11.1.

11.6 No cash compensation is provided for domestic cancellations or delays beyond refunds under this section and the assistance described in the Meal and Hotel Assistance Policy (CA-CC-POL-021). Goodwill travel credits may be issued only by Tier 2 Customer Care Specialists in accordance with CA-CC-GDE-007.

## 12. Customer-Requested Changes Versus Carrier Disruptions

12.1 Customer-requested changes are governed by the fare rules of the ticket.

12.2 Since January 1, 2025, no change fee applies to Main Cabin and Economy Plus fares for flights within the United States. The fare difference between the original fare and the new fare applies.

12.3 Basic fares cannot be changed.

12.4 A same-day confirmed change carries a fee of US$75 per passenger. The fee is waived for Platinum members in accordance with CA-LOY-POL-003. Gold members receive a waiver of the same-day standby fee in accordance with CA-LOY-POL-003.

12.5 Customer-requested changes are not disruptions. They do not qualify for the change-fee waiver in Section 6, for refunds under Section 11.1, or for meal or hotel assistance.

12.6 A change requested before a disruption occurs is a customer-requested change. If the booked flight is later affected by a carrier-initiated cancellation or significant delay, the customer may then be reaccommodated under this policy.

12.7 When Contoso Air publishes a travel advisory before a forecast event, such as severe weather, the terms of that advisory determine whether fees are waived. Customer Care must not extend an advisory beyond its published dates, airports or conditions.

## 13. Significant Delays and Schedule Changes

13.1 A delay is significant when the departure is delayed by 3 hours or more compared with the scheduled departure time on the passenger's itinerary. Customer Care must use the current estimated departure time in the operational system to determine whether a delay is significant.

13.2 When a flight is subject to a significant delay, the passenger may remain on the delayed flight, be reaccommodated on an eligible alternative without a change fee or fare difference, or decline all eligible alternatives and request a refund under Section 11.

13.3 If the estimated delay later decreases to less than 3 hours, a completed reaccommodation remains valid.

13.4 A schedule change is a qualifying schedule change when it moves the scheduled departure or arrival time by 3 hours or more, or when it converts a nonstop itinerary into a connecting itinerary. A qualifying schedule change is handled in the same way as a significant delay for the purposes of Sections 6, 7, 11 and 13.2.

13.5 A schedule change that does not meet Section 13.4 is not a qualifying schedule change. However, if any schedule change reduces a connection below the minimum connection time, the passenger must be reaccommodated on an eligible alternative without a change fee or fare difference.

13.6 For delays of less than 3 hours, Customer Care should give the current estimated departure time and advise the customer to monitor flight notifications. Meal and hotel eligibility is determined solely under CA-CC-POL-021.

## 14. Escalation Conditions

14.1 Tier 1 Customer Care must escalate in accordance with the Customer Service Escalation Guide (CA-CC-GDE-007) when any of the following conditions applies:

- no satisfactory same-day eligible alternative is available, or no eligible alternative accommodates the whole traveling party;
- the customer asks to split the traveling party;
- a passenger has accessibility requirements, such as a wheelchair, a mobility device or a service animal;
- the customer reports a special circumstance, such as a medical condition affecting travel, a bereavement, travel with an infant requiring specific arrangements, or a passenger at risk of being stranded without means to wait for the next eligible alternative;
- the reservation includes an unaccompanied minor;
- the customer raises an international travel documentation concern;
- the reservation record and the operational flight status conflict;
- the customer requests compensation, cash, a free upgrade, a fee waiver beyond this policy, a travel credit, rebooking on another airline or any other action outside Tier 1 authority; or
- reservation or operational systems are unavailable and the required information cannot be confirmed.

14.2 When escalating, Customer Care must tell the customer which team will handle the request and give the receiving team a summary so that the customer does not need to repeat information.

## 15. Roles and Responsibilities

| Role | Responsibilities |
|---|---|
| Tier 1 Customer Care | Determine eligibility, present eligible alternatives, complete agreed rebookings, explain refund eligibility and escalate under Section 14. |
| Tier 2 Customer Care | Handle escalated cases, complete customer-chosen splits and issue goodwill travel credits within CA-CC-GDE-007 limits. |
| IROPS Recovery Desk | Manage irregular operations, verify conflicting records and arrange options outside the eligible-alternative definition, including other airlines. |
| Refunds team | Process refund requests. |
| Airport customer service | Reaccommodate passengers at the airport and issue meal and hotel vouchers. |

## 16. Exceptions

16.1 Exceptions may be authorized only by the Director, Customer Care Policy, a Customer Care Manager, or the IROPS Recovery Desk duty manager during declared irregular operations, and must be recorded in the reservation notes with the reason.

16.2 Loyalty status, repeated contacts, or a statement that another representative promised a different outcome do not by themselves justify an exception.
